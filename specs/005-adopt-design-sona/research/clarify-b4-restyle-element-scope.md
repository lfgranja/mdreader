# Clarification B4 — Restyle element scope: strict enumeration vs. keep-and-restyle

**Scope:** single in-app driving surface of `mdreader` (StatusView grade 2-campo) plus the MediaStyle notification, both glance surfaces. Question: when an existing on-screen element is not in the design spec's enumerated set (e.g. section table-of-contents, elapsed/remaining time, extras the current build shows), does the restyle (a) canonicalize the screen to only the enumerated elements, or (b) keep-and-restyle whatever exists?

**Recommendation up front:** (a) — strict enumeration / canonicalize. Non-enumerated elements must be *removed* from the driving surface (not merely hidden), and any genuinely useful extra (TOC, elapsed time) must be gated to a parked/passenger surface or delivered eyes-free (voice/earcon), never carried as a styled-but-unused field on a glance surface.

## 1. Visual-manual glance standards (NHTSA / ISO / SAE)

**NHTSA (2013)** *Visual-Manual NHTSA Driver Distraction Guidelines for In-Vehicle Electronic Devices*, 78 FR 24817 (Apr. 26, 2013). Applies to original-equipment in-vehicle devices used for secondary (non-driving-related) visual-manual tasks. Acceptance criteria: a task must be completable with single glances away from the roadway of **≤ 2.0 s** and cumulative eyes-off-road time of **≤ 12.0 s** (eye-glance driving simulator protocol: 85% of glances < 2.0 s; mean glance < 2.0 s; total < 12.0 s). The Guidelines also enumerate tasks that **must not be available to the driver while driving** at all, including: displaying video not related to driving; displaying certain graphical/photographic images; **displaying automatically scrolling text**; **manual text entry for messaging/communication/browsing**; and **displaying text for reading from books, periodical publications, web pages, social media, advertising or text messages** (NHTSA 2013, §IV; Federal Register 2013). A driving audio app's "now playing" surface is a secondary visual-manual task; any element that invites reading (section TOC, body text, elapsed/remaining time as a scannable string) is exactly the class of content the Guidelines prohibit while the vehicle moves.

**ISO 15008:2017** *Road vehicles — Ergonomic aspects of transport information and control systems — Specifications and test procedures for in-vehicle visual presentation.* Specifies the visual presentation of transport information to the driver of a passenger car while in motion: character dimensions (minimum 12 arcmin, recommended ≥ 20 arcmin; 16 arcmin acceptable), contrast, luminance, colour, pixel format, reflections/glare, image instability/flashing. Its scope is perceptual (legibility), and it is the authority on whether text on a glance surface can be read at all at driving viewing distances — i.e. even "harmless" small text must meet the size floor or be removed.

**ISO 15007-1:2014** *Road vehicles — Measurement of driver visual behaviour with respect to transport information and control systems — Part 1: Definitions and parameters.* Defines glance duration and the measurement protocol behind the 2 s / 12 s criteria; the metric by which any restyled surface must be re-validated.

**ISO 16673:2007** (occlusion method) and **SAE J2364** (eye-glance measurement) are the companion test methods NHTSA references for the same criteria.

**Note on ISO 25741:** as published by ISO, ISO/TR 25741 concerns lifts/escalators (seismic compilation report), not in-vehicle displays; it is not applicable here. The relevant in-vehicle display standard set is ISO 15008 (presentation) + ISO 15007-1 (visual behaviour) + ISO 16673 (occlusion) + SAE J2364, cited above.

**Parked vs. driving.** NHTSA's Phase 2 (portable/aftermarket, 2016) and Google's Design for Driving both distinguish *driving state* from *parked/passenger state*: tasks that fail the glance criteria (or fall in the prohibited list) are permitted only when the vehicle is parked. Google's media template documentation explicitly separates "View more list text while parked" as a parked-only flow (Google Design for Driving, Media apps; "Parked & passenger apps"). Therefore an element like a section TOC or elapsed/remaining time is not forbidden outright — it is forbidden *on the driving surface*; it belongs to a parked/passenger surface or to a voice/earcon channel.

## 2. Clutter / visual-search evidence: extra elements hurt glance performance

- **Lee, Kim & Ji (2019)** *Effects of visual complexity of in-vehicle information display: Age-related differences in visual search task in the driving context*, Applied Ergonomics 81:102888 (doi:10.1016/j.apergo.2019.102888). Visual complexity level of the in-vehicle display had a significant effect on driving behaviour, **especially in older drivers**; higher complexity → longer time-on-task and more fixations. This is a direct, in-vehicle result: adding fields to a glance surface degrades the primary task.

- **Lee, Kim & Ji (2016)** *Perceived Visual Complexity of In-Vehicle Information Display and Its Effects on Glance Behavior and Preferences*, International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction 32(7) (doi:10.1080/10447318.2016.1184546). High perceived visual complexity → more time-on-task and number of fixations; preference was **inversely proportional** to complexity. Keeping an unused-but-styled field raises perceived complexity even if the driver never reads it, because it increases search set size.

- **Edquist (2010)** *The effects of visual clutter on driving performance*, Monash University thesis (TRID 898570). Visual clutter affects driver workload and hazard perception/search; roadside clutter is treated at a comparable danger level to in-vehicle device distraction. The mechanism (more items → more competition for visual attention) transfers to in-vehicle layouts.

- **Wickens' Multiple Resource Theory** (Wickens 2008, *Human Factors* 50(3):449–455; Horrey & Wickens 2006, *J. Exp. Psychol.: Applied* 12(2):67–78). Driving and a visual-manual in-vehicle task draw on the same visual/spatial resource pool; competition degrades both. Removing non-essential fields removes resource competition — it is not cosmetic.

- **Visual-search set-size effect** (Treisman & Gelade 1980, *Cognitive Psychology* 12:97–136; Wolfe 1998, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 2(9):323–329; Wolfe 2010, *Annual Review of Psychology* 61:169–192; Duncan & Humphreys 1989, *Psychological Review* 96:433–458). Search time grows with the number of items in the display (serial/conjunction search ~20–50 ms/item; even pop-out search shows set-size costs under clutter). Every extra field on a glance surface is a distractor that must be rejected before the target (title/section/progress) is found. A field that is "only restyled" still occupies pixels and still competes.

- **Allianz Center for Technology (2026)** found that **identical tasks added up to 3 s of off-road glance time depending on cockpit/interface design** — i.e. layout alone, independent of task content, moves a design across the NHTSA 2 s / 12 s boundary. This is the strongest applied evidence that "keep-and-restyle" (changing colours/fonts but keeping the element count) is not neutral: it preserves the excess glance cost.

**Hidden vs. removed.** A CSS-hidden element still exists in the DOM, still receives layout, and is one toggle away from being shown again; it also tends to be re-enabled later ("it's already there, just un-hide it"). For a safety-critical glance surface the discipline that matches the standards is structural removal from the driving template, with the extra content relocated to a parked/passenger surface or an eyes-free channel. Google's "View more list text while parked" pattern is the model: the richer content is a *different surface*, gated by driving state, not a hidden block on the same screen.

## 3. Industry practice: what a minimal "now playing" surface contains

- **Google Design for Driving — Media Playback template** (developers.google.com/cars/design, Media apps): the driving "now playing" surface shows the now-playing item (title/artist, artwork) plus transport controls (play/pause, next/previous, overflow). Optional additions (queue, thumbnails, elapsed time for the now-playing item, previously played items) are *design decisions the developer makes*, and richer browsing (sectioned item lists, queue review) is a separate, parked/passenger-capable surface. Google explicitly provides a "View more list text while parked" flow, signalling that list/TOC-style content is parked-gated.
- **NHTSA (2013)** prohibited list (reading text, scrolling text) is the reason Google's media template keeps the driving surface to metadata + transport + progress; elapsed/remaining time and TOC are not part of the glance-critical set.
- **Podcast/audiobook automotive conventions** (implied by the same template and by Android Auto/AAOS media UX requirements) likewise limit the driving surface to title, (optional) subtitle/section, progress and transport; chapter/TOC navigation happens in a browsing view that is appropriate to the parked state. This matches mdreader's own contract (`contracts/surface.md`): lock-screen and in-app fields are exactly title + section title + progress + transport (+ Stop in-app, value-blind rate stepper); "Never: document prose, elapsed/remaining time, playback rate, voice identity."

## 4. Recommendation

Adopt **(a) strict enumeration / canonicalize** for the driving surface:

1. **Remove** any element not in the enumerated set from the driving template (StatusView + notification), rather than restyling or hiding it. The enumerated set for mdreader is already fixed by `contracts/surface.md` and FR-002/FR-008 of spec 005: title, current section, progress, transport controls, value-blind rate stepper, Stop (in-app), reason line (TTL 3 s). Anything else (section TOC/outline drawer, elapsed/remaining time, numeric rate, voice identity, body text, brand marks, RMS strip, buffer ring) is out of scope for this surface.

2. **Relocate, don't delete, useful extras**: a section TOC or elapsed/remaining time, if wanted at all, goes to a parked/passenger surface (a future feature with its own driving-state gate) or to an eyes-free channel (voice/earcon), per Google's parked-gating pattern and NHTSA Phase 2. They must not be carried as styled-but-unused fields.

3. **Treat "not enumerated" as a MUST NOT** in the spec (not a "not currently shown"): the purity/inspection checks (SC-001, SC-006 closed-hex set) should be extended to element inventory, so a future edit cannot silently re-add a field by restyling it. This is the same fail-closed discipline already applied to colour and to the closed rate ladder.

4. **Why not (b)**: keep-and-restyle preserves the exact glance cost the standards target (Lee 2019/2016; Allianz 2026), keeps prohibited content (NHTSA 2013 text-for-reading list) one toggle away from the driver, and converts a safety property into a styling preference. Restyling is not free of safety effect when the element count is unchanged.

## Implications for the spec

- **FR-002 / FR-008 / contracts/surface.md** already state the enumerated field set and the "Never" list; this research supports making the "Never" list **structural** (element absent from the driving template) rather than presentational (hidden/unused). The restyle task should delete the non-enumerated elements from `status_view.html` and the notification builder, not just re-token them.
- **SC-001 / SC-006** (closed-set inspection) should add an **element-inventory check** alongside the closed-hex check: the driving surface's DOM/notification fields must equal the enumerated set; any additional field fails the check regardless of colour.
- **Parked/passenger content** (section TOC/outline drawer, elapsed/remaining time, numeric rate, RMS strip, buffer ring, telemetry matrix) is deferred to a future feature that declares a driving-state gate (parked/passenger) and its own glance validation; it must not ship on the driving surface in this feature, even restyled.
- **ISO 25741** is not an in-vehicle display standard; cite ISO 15008 + ISO 15007-1 + ISO 16673 + SAE J2364 instead in any spec reference.
- **No spec change to the enumerated set itself** is implied by this research: the existing enumeration (title, section, progress, transport, value-blind rate stepper, Stop in-app, 3 s reason line) is consistent with NHTSA 2013, ISO 15008, Google's media template, and the clutter literature. The change is in how non-enumerated elements are treated: removed, not restyled.
