# Clarification E3 — SC-002 Haptic Side-Channel (Audio Gate vs. Bimodal Stimulation)

> **Feature:** `specs/005-adopt-design-sona` (SC-002: seven confirmation types blind-identified on-device, incl. skip-direction sub-score)
> **Question:** SC-002 asks blind listeners to identify the synthesized earcons by ear, but the shipped app fires a *redundant* vibrotactile confirmation on every transport action with asymmetric patterns (20+40 ms vs 40+20 ms for skip-fwd/back). Should haptics be ON (ecological reproduction of the multimodal UX), DISABLED/neutralized (audio-channel isolation), or run as a DUAL condition — and, if isolated, should a separate haptic identification leg be run?
> **Scope:** research note for the maintainer-driver single-user gate; not a population claim; informs a clarification decision only.
> **Method:** primary-source review of multisensory perception (redundant-target effect, cross-modal enhancement), earcon-evaluation practice (Brewster/McGookin, ISO 7731/9241-126), vibrotactile psychophysics (two-pulse pattern identification, temporal-order judgments), in-vehicle HMI/accessibility guidance (NHTSA 2013), and Google/Android haptics principles. 2026-10-24.

## 1. The confound: why bimodal stimulation threatens the audio gate

The shipped app publishes a closed seven-pattern haptic table (`android-shell/.../MediaMirror.kt`: Play 30 ms, Pause 80 ms, SkipFwd 20+40 ms, SkipBack 40+20 ms, Warning 15+15+30 ms, Failure 80+80 ms, RateStep 10 ms), selected by Rust per action and rendered by the mirror (FR-016). SC-002, as amended by clarification B1, demands blind identification of **seven** types — play, pause, skip-fwd, skip-back, rate, warning, failure — with a **direction sub-score** for the two skips, on the reference device's media speaker in a parked car.

Because the trial administers the *real* app on the reference device, every stimulus is **bimodal**: audio earcon + haptic pattern, same onset, same action. Two consequences:

1. **Skip direction is recoverable from touch alone.** The two haptic waveforms are exact temporal mirrors (20+40 vs 40+20 ms). A participant who feels the vibration can score the direction sub-score without hearing anything.
2. **Type identity is also recoverable from touch alone.** Play (30), Pause (80), Rate (10), Warning (triple), Failure (80+80) are distinct haptic codes; a participant could in principle map all seven from vibration.

Therefore a bimodal trial **cannot attribute success to the audio channel**. The claim the spec actually wants — "the SOUND was identified" — would be unfalsifiable under condition A. This is the textbook *redundant-signals confound*: a correlated non-target modality carries the target information.

## 2. Cross-modal redundancy increases identification accuracy

- **Redundant-target effect (RTE).** Multisensory integration theory and experiment show that redundant congruent cues across modalities speed responses and, in identification tasks, raise accuracy (Wikipedia, *Multisensory integration* — redundant-target effect; Wang et al. 2023, *Perception* 52(2): "Modal-based attention modulates the redundant-signals effect" — RSE improves both speed and accuracy for bimodal audiovisual targets vs. unimodal; Hagmann & Russo 2016, *Atten. Percept. Psychophys.* 78(8): multisensory integration of redundant trisensory stimulation).
- **Cross-modal perceptual enhancement.** Bimodal presentation can improve unisensory target performance even when the second modality is nominally "ignored" (Ball et al. 2022, *Vision Res.* 193:108003, "Cross-modal perceptual enhancement of unisensory targets"). The second modality need not be attended to help.
- **Audiotactile assimilation.** Touch and audition interact symmetrically: the frequency in the attended modality is perceived as similar to the unattended one (Convento et al. 2019; reviewed in Casado-Palacios et al. 2025, *Brain Sci.* 15(3):288, "Cross-Modal Interactions of the Tactile System", PMC9674209). Even an "ignored" vibration biases auditory identification.
- **Earcon-design doctrine on segregation.** Brewster's guidelines state earcons can be presented in parallel to raise information transfer **only when segregated** (unique timbre, spatialization, onset asynchrony); without segregation they fuse/interfere (Brewster, Wright & Edwards 1993, *INTERCHI '93* pp. 222–227; Brewster 1994, *Int. J. Human-Computer Studies* 43:153–175; McGookin & Brewster 2004, *ACM Trans. Applied Perception* 1(2):130–155). In this app audio and haptic share onset and action — they are **not** segregated, so they fuse rather than act as independent channels.

**Implication:** a bimodal SC-002 run measures a *multisensory* percept, not the audio earcon. The gate that claims "sound identified" must be unimodal.

## 3. Methodological guidance: unimodal isolation in recognition measurement

- **Auditory-display practice.** Earcon identification studies evaluate the auditory channel in isolation; concurrent redundant modalities are treated as a separate experimental variable, never the default (Sonification Handbook ch. 14, McGookin & Brewster; ICAD conference practice). Bonebright & Nees 2009, *Appl. Cognit. Psychol.* 23(3):431–445 shows earcons do not impair spoken-passage comprehension — supporting additive mixing with *speech*, not with a second structured signal.
- **ISO 7731:2003** (danger signals): the subjective listening test is an audibility-over-noise (masked-threshold) test with the signal reaching the ear; the transducer is part of the system under test. **ISO/TS 9241-126:2019** §7.5.1/§9.5 give duration guidance. Neither standard prescribes multimodal presentation; alarm evaluation treats the auditory channel as the unit under test. A concurrent tactile channel would be an uncontrolled variable.
- **McGookin & Brewster 2004 (ACM TAP):** concurrent earcons interfere unless separated by ~300 ms or made spectrally distinct — the load-bearing finding that motivates treating *any* simultaneous structured second signal as a confound to be removed or modeled.
- **Sensory-evaluation norm.** When a non-target modality is present and correlated with the target, it must be either **removed** or **explicitly modeled as a condition**. A single bimodal run cannot yield a unimodal verdict; the unimodal condition is the control.

## 4. Vibrotactile pattern identification reliability (can a separate haptic leg be valid?)

If haptics are isolated out of the audio gate, the natural follow-up is a **separate haptic identification leg**. Its validity depends on:

- **Temporal resolution of touch.** Touch JNDs are significantly larger than auditory ones: a Springer *Atten. Percept. Psychophys.* 2021 study ("Perceptual timing precision with vibrotactile, auditory, and multisensory stimuli") reports tactile JNDs higher than auditory and audio-tactile JNDs. Two-pulse and temporal-order tasks require longer separations in touch than in hearing.
- **Two-pulse pattern identification.** Sequential presentation is vastly more effective than simultaneous for accuracy (Yeganeh et al. 2023, *Appl. Sci.* 14(1):43); short patterns (2–3 activations) outperform long ones. For two-pulse patterns, accuracy peaks at ISIs around 50–700 ms (Yeganeh et al. 2025, "Vibrotactile pattern recognition: influence of interstimulus interval"). The shipped haptic patterns use a **fixed 20 ms gap** between buzzes — far below the range where two-pulse accuracy peaks. This is a validity risk for a standalone haptic leg and argues for pilot testing before committing SC-004 to 10/10.
- **Minimum pulse duration.** Kobayashi's work on rhythmic vibrotactile contexts places the minimum perceivable stimulus duration around **50 ms**. The shipped table contains buzzes of 10 ms (RateStep) and 15 ms (Warning components) — below that floor, so those buzzes may not be reliably perceived as distinct pulses on all devices/locations. Again a validity risk for the haptic leg.
- **Audiotactile temporal order.** Spence et al. 2003 (*J. Exp. Psychol. Hum. Percept. Perform.* 29(3):755–775, "Multisensory temporal order judgments") and Zampini et al. 2005 (*Percept. Psychophys.* 67(3):424–436, "Audiotactile temporal order judgments") show the point of subjective simultaneity is shifted so that touch must lead sound by tens of ms to be perceived as simultaneous. A "simultaneous" audio+haptic stimulus is therefore **not** perceived as simultaneous; the haptic can be processed as a separate event — another reason the audio gate must be isolated, and a reason a bimodal condition can actually *aid* identification beyond either channel alone.
- **Off-body/stereo rendering stability.** Phone-motor output varies by device and body location (Kappers & Plaisier 2022, "Guidance for the Design of Vibrotactile Patterns"; abdomen/back 2D-pattern studies show spatial/temporal influences). A separate haptic leg is valid **only if** run on the same reference device, same body location (hand/pocket), with its own familiarization phase. It should be reported as a **separate acceptance criterion** (SC-004's domain — "10/10 blind per confirmation type, sounds on and off"), not folded into SC-002.

## 5. Google / Material haptics guidance

- **Android haptics principles** (developer.android.com, "Haptics design principles"): "We strongly recommend co-design of visual, audio, and haptic effects. Make it harmonious or congruent with visual animations and sound patterns. Visual and auditory inputs can enhance the haptics perceived, and a well-designed haptic effect can provide a sense of physicality to visual and audio effects." → Google explicitly treats haptics as part of a **multimodal whole** and acknowledges **cross-modal enhancement**. This supports ecological (bimodal) testing for UX validation, but it is the *opposite* of an isolation claim.
- **Material "Good Vibrations"** (design.google): multisensory design is holistic; haptics are composed *with* sound and vision. Google guidance prescribes **co-design and congruence**, not independent measurement.
- **Conclusion:** Google's position supports bimodal UX but **undermines** any unimodal audio attribution. It argues for a **dual-condition** design, not a single bimodal gate.

## 6. Ecological validity and dual-condition design

- **Ecological argument.** The driver does feel both in the shipped app; testing only audio understates real-world performance but overstates the audio channel's standalone contribution. These are **different questions** and both are legitimate.
- **Multi-condition norms.** Standard psychophysical practice (constant-stimulus method; Clarke 1957, *J. Acoust. Soc. Am.* 29:715–720) compares conditions; when a redundant cue is present, the unimodal condition is the **control**. No external standard mandates either option here.
- **NHTSA.** The 2013 Visual-Manual NHTSA Driver Distraction Guidelines set glance-time bounds (≤2 s single glance, ≤12 s total task) and task-completion limits; they **do not prescribe multimodal notification evaluation** or an identification gate. They address visual-manual distraction, not auditory identification. So no external standard forces the choice; it is a spec-level decision.

## 7. Options

- **A — Keep haptics ON in the trial.** Cost: none (no protocol change). Risk: invalidates audio attribution; direction sub-score is trivially passable from touch; SC-002 cannot claim sound distinctiveness. **Not recommended** for the gate.
- **B — Disable/neutralize haptics for the audio gate + optional separate haptic leg.** Cost: medium (disable vibrator in the test build or use a haptics-off configuration; add haptic leg). Benefit: clean audio attribution; SC-002 measures what it claims. **Recommended as the gate** if budget is tight.
- **C — Run TWO conditions (bimodal + audio-only) and gate on the audio-only leg.** Cost: highest (~2× trials; two sessions or interleaved blocks with counterbalanced order). Benefit: preserves ecological data **and** clean attribution; quantifies the redundancy gain. **Recommended if feasible** (maintainer is N=1 on one device; roughly one extra session). Otherwise B.

**Recommendation:** C if feasible, else B. Never A for the gate. The haptic leg must be a **separate acceptance criterion** (SC-004 territory) with its own familiarization and device/location controls — not folded into SC-002.

## Implication for the spec

SC-002's gate condition must be stated as **audio-only (haptics disabled/neutralized)** on the reference device's media speaker for the unimodal verdict; the bimodal condition is a secondary/ecological condition; haptic identification belongs to SC-004 with its own trial. The protocol must add an explicit haptics-disabled control and a note that the app's haptic patterns are a redundant channel that must not be present during the audio gate. The confusion matrix (B1) gains a **condition column** (audio-only vs. bimodal), and any redundancy-gain magnitude is recorded as a measured quantity, not a pass/fail. A failed audio-only gate is the evidence that authorizes revising the earcon grammar (per B1) — never a silent edit.

## Correction (2026-10-24, post-dive) — accepted resolution (batch E, option A)

The maintainer accepted the recommended gate behavior with a cheaper reading of it. The DIVE (same date) found the shipped binary's haptics already DORMANT — Rust publishes `haptic=None` (`lib.rs:6047`) and the 7-pattern table in `MediaMirror.kt` never fires — so "disable/neutralize" is the shipped state, not a build change. Accepted disposition: SC-002's gate condition is **audio-only by attribution** (app haptics inert), the trial sheet gains an explicit **haptic-disposition line** (rigid mount, hands off, administrator verifies silence-of-buzz at the pilot), and the haptic half belongs to 004's T051-04 sounds-off condition — no separate haptic leg inside SC-002. §7's option labels are superseded by the batch presentation (batch E: **A** = sound-only gate; **B** = dual-trial bimodal leg; **C** = wiring haptics in this feature; **D** = no clause): B/C rejected (cross the 004 contract boundary; restyle-only mandate) and D rejected (leaves the disposition undefined if 004's haptic wiring lands before the trial — the disposition then requires its own deactivation route, build flag or publish `None`).
