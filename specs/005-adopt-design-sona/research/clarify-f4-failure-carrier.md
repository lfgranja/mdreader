# Clarification F4 — Failure-state carrier and clearing transitions for the permanent red signal

> **Feature:** `specs/005-adopt-design-sona` (FR-001/FR-005/FR-008/SC-001; candidate clarification F4)
> **Question:** F4 pins permanent failure to red `--signal-failure` `#FF4D63` as "static paint, no pulse" (E2), but leaves open (i) which in-app surface element carries the red (progress track/fill vs. reason banner vs. a dot) and (ii) which transitions clear it (next play? new document? restart? timeout?). The 004 contract's reason line expires after 3 s (warning TTL); persistent failures use `reason_ttl_ms=null` and outlive that, so in-app failure-indicator persistence is the open design question.
> **Scope:** research note; not a spec amendment. Informs a clarification decision only.
> **Method (2026-10-24):** Re-verification of the three papers already cited in this repo's notes (Sarter & Woods 1995; Parasuraman & Riley 1997; Bliss & Acton 2003) plus ISO 15006 temporal classes; FMVSS No. 101 tell-tale rules (primary, eCFR); NHTSA 812360 human-factors guidance (direction only — PDF 403 this pass); W3C Understanding 4.1.3 Status Messages; Material 3 error color roles; IEC 60601-1-8 latching vs. non-latching alarm convention (medical-device analogue); alarm-fatigue origin literature (EBSCO/Joint Commission). pt-BR spec terms quoted verbatim.

## 1. What is already pinned (and what is not)

- **Pinned (E2/D2):** permanent failure = static red `#FF4D63`, no pulse, no motion >5 s (else 2.2.2). Red is exclusive to the failure state; never adjacent to yellow (2.25:1 mutual contrast). Notification paints no state colour (accent-only `--sona-voice`); states ride the reason line + earcon + TTL.
- **Pinned (004 contract):** persistent failures (`VoiceUnavailable`/`FormatUndeliverable`/`DecodeFailed`) carry `reason_ttl_ms=null` — the reason line never auto-expires on either surface. Transient warnings (focus/device) carry `reason_ttl_ms=3000`.
- **Pinned (004 spec FR-009):** failure ends the session as `Interrupted` (EndKind); "wait paused with an explicit resume control"; narration never auto-resumes; the Sforzando earcon fires immediately at the state change and is never queued/dropped.
- **Not pinned (F4):** (a) the in-app surface element that paints red — progress track/fill vs. reason banner vs. a dot; (b) the transition that clears it — next successful play, new document load, app restart, or a timeout (which would contradict `reason_ttl_ms=null`).

## 2. Persistent vs. brief error signalling — the evidence

- **Sarter & Woods (1995)**, *Human Factors* 37:5–19, "How in the World Did We Get into That Mode?" (https://doi.org/10.1518/001872095779049461): poor feedback about automation state is a principal contributor to mode error; the recurring operator questions are "What is it doing? Why? What next? How did we get into that mode?" They recommend auditory/kinetic clues and "displaying recent data" as mode-awareness aids because the visual field is already occupied. A failure that vanishes after 3 s leaves the driver unable to answer "what happened?" — the persistent red + reason line is the "recent data" Sarter & Woods call for.
- **Parasuraman & Riley (1997)**, *Human Factors* 39(2):230–253, "Humans and Automation" (https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886): inadequate feedback drives *misuse* (over-reliance) and *disuse* (under-utilization); trust, workload and risk shape whether the operator uses the automation at all. A confirmation channel that sometimes produces nothing and never says so is exactly that failure mode — hence a persistent, unambiguous failure indication beats a self-clearing one.
- **Bliss & Acton (2003)**, *Applied Ergonomics* 34(6):507–516, "Alarm mistrust in automobiles" (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apergo.2003.07.003): with 70 simulator drivers, alarm and swerving reactions improved monotonically with reliability (50/75/100%); design imperative: **maximize alarm reliability while minimizing invasiveness**. The Bliss/cry-wolf line (Breznitz 1983; probability-matching ≈90% of subjects) shows operators *calibrate* to perceived reliability — a channel that sometimes silently fails trains the driver to discount it. A persistent red that self-clears after N seconds (a "warning TTL" masquerading as a failure) would be read as low reliability; a red that persists until the condition resolves is honest.
- **Endsley (1995)**, *Human Factors* 37(1):32–64, "Toward a Theory of Situation Awareness in Dynamic Systems" (https://doi.org/10.1518/001872095779049281): Level-2/3 SA (comprehension/projection) requires that the *cause* of a state change remain available while the operator is not looking at the display; a transient 3 s flash is a Level-1 (perception) cue only. Permanent failures need a persistent Level-2 cue — the red + reason line — until the operator re-engages.
- **ISO 15006:2011** (https://www.iso.org/standard/55322.html) temporal classes: short-term 0–3 s, medium 3–10 s, longer-term >10 s. A permanent engine/audio failure is a *longer-term* condition; ISO 15006 mandates short-term signals be sent "immediately after a critical event is detected" but its classes imply that a condition outliving 10 s is no longer a short-term warning — it needs a persistent (not self-expiring) indication. ISO 15006 is auditory; the visual analogue is the tell-tale (§4).
- **IEC 60601-1-8** latching vs. non-latching convention (medical-device analogue, not normative for this app): non-latching alarms are "automatically removed when the alarm condition no longer exists"; latching alarms "remain active even when the alarm condition no longer exists" and require an explicit reset/acknowledgment (Philips HeartStart/Efficia IFU, https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/Instruction%20for%20Use/20241219/5d4c8b57149f42ecae4eb24b01740a7a.pdf). A permanent failure whose condition *never* auto-resolves (engine dead until re-open) is latching by construction; the "reset" is the next successful playback start.

## 3. Alarm fatigue / habituation from permanently-lit red

- **Origin case (EBSCO Research Starters, "Alarm Fatigue", https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/environmental-sciences/alarm-fatigue):** early ventilator monitors used continuously blinking lights to signify normal function and burning risk; "because the lights blinked continually, nurses became desensitized to the signal and failed to notice risk indicators." The Joint Commission (2013, https://www.jointcommission.org) logged 98 alarm-fatigue-related sentinel events 2009–2012. The lesson: a *continuously lit/blinking* red trains non-response; a red that is *rare, state-locked, and cleared by the next successful action* does not.
- **Bliss & Acton (2003)** (above) + **Parasuraman & Riley (1997)** (above): operators calibrate to perceived reliability; a channel that cries wolf or never clears loses trust. Mitigation for this app: the red is (a) session-scoped (cleared by next successful play/new doc/restart), (b) genuinely rare (engine failure, device failure, refused start, decode failure — not a high-frequency event), (c) small-area (a dot, not a fill, per Material/Wear "saturated colours only as small accents"), and (d) multi-channel (reason line + Sforzando earcon + TalkBack status region — never colour alone, per NHTSA 812360 and Carbon's "color + shape + symbol" rule). The red dot is therefore a latching, not a blinking, signal — and E2 already forbids the pulse.

## 4. In-vehicle specific — tell-tale conventions (primary source)

- **FMVSS No. 101, §5.5.6(b)** (49 CFR 571.101, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-571/subpart-B/section-571.101): "**Telltales for high beams, turn signal, low tire pressure, and passenger air bag off, and telltales for which the color red is required in table 1 to this section must not be cancelable while the underlying condition for their activation exists.**" Direct, primary, verifiable: a red tell-tale must **stay illuminated while the underlying condition exists** and **cannot be dismissed/cleared by the driver mid-condition**. It clears only when the condition resolves. Also §5.3.1(e): "A telltale must not emit light except when identifying the malfunction or vehicle condition it is designed to indicate, or during a bulb check" — i.e., the lamp is on *iff* the condition exists; no decorative persistence.
- **NHTSA 812360, Human Factors Design Guidance for Driver-Vehicle Interfaces** (https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/documents/812360_humanfactorsdesignguidance.pdf — 403 this pass; direction already cited in D2/D5): amber = advisory/cautionary, red = imminent warnings; colour is redundant with shape/text and is never the sole channel. Consistent with FR-005/SC-004 (mute-mode operability) and with the notification's accent-only rule.
- **ISO 15008:2017** (road vehicles, in-vehicle visual presentation; paywalled — direction only, as in D2): treats colour as a per-element legibility parameter under three illumination conditions; prescribes no hex values. No numeric claim quoted as authoritative.
- **UNECE R121 / FMVSS 101 tell-tale tables** (secondary, via the eCFR Table 1 colour column and the UK consolidated list of compulsory UNECE regulations): red tell-tales = malfunction/failure, yellow = caution, green = normal/active. Malfunction indicator lamps (MIL) are the automotive analogue of "permanent failure": they illuminate on fault and clear on resolution, not on driver cancellation. (Marked secondary: no UNECE text retrieved this pass; the FMVSS 101 citation above is the primary, verifiable anchor.)

## 5. W3C / Material 3 — persistence and announcement semantics

- **WCAG SC 4.1.3 Status Messages (Level AA)** (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html): a status message "provides information … on the existence of errors"; for a *persisting* status message, "modifications to this text are usually equivalent to a new status message"; and "in situations where status text is entirely removed, its absence may itself convey information … non-sighted users would be unaware of this change, unless the end of the waiting state results in a change of context." Implication: if the red clears, the *clearing* must itself be announced (or be a change of context) — otherwise a screen-reader user never learns the failure resolved. Conversely, a persistent failure that *never* clears in-app (latching) avoids the "silent removal" problem entirely; the resolution is communicated by the new session's Play succeeding (a change of context with its own announcement).
- **Material 3 color roles** (https://m3.material.io/styles/color/roles): `error`/`errorContainer`/`onError`/`onErrorContainer` are first-class roles with separate light/dark values; Material ships red as a "static color" example (https://m3.material.io/styles/color/advanced/define-new-colors). M3 treats error as a *role* (persistent while the error condition holds), not a transient flash — consistent with a latching red dot that clears on resolution, not on a timer.

## 6. Options compared

**Carrier (in-app element that paints red):**

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Progress track/fill | Reject | Conflates position (progress) with state (failure); the fill is `--sona-voice` white in both determinate/indeterminate (C1/C5); painting it red would lie about progress and break the fill-token invariant. |
| Reason banner text | Reject as sole carrier | The banner already carries the reason *words* + is typed `role="status"` (D5); painting the whole text red hurts legibility (halation, §4 of D2) and double-encodes a message already given by words+earcon. At most a small leading glyph, never the text. |
| Static red dot (analogous to the yellow pulsing dot) | **Adopt** | Mirrors the established yellow-dot affordance (D2: yellow pulsing dot = transient warning; red static dot = permanent failure); small-area accent (Wear/Material "saturated only as small accents"); no pulse (E2, 2.2.2); preserves the closed element inventory (no new element — same dot slot, different colour/state). |

**Clearing transition:**

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Timeout (e.g., 3 s TTL) | Reject | Contradicts `reason_ttl_ms=null` for persistent failures and FMVSS 101 §5.5.6(b) (red tell-tales must not be cancelable while the condition exists); a self-clearing red reads as low reliability (Bliss & Acton) and silently drops the failure (SC 4.1.3 removal problem). |
| Next successful Play on the same document | **Adopt (primary)** | Condition resolves → indicator clears, mirroring FMVSS 101 "illuminated only while the condition exists" and IEC 60601-1-8 non-latching clearing; the Play itself is a change of context that announces resolution to AT. |
| New document load | Adopt (secondary) | A new session resets all state; clears trivially. |
| App restart | Adopt (tertiary) | Fresh process state; the diagnostic record (30-day JSONL) still carries the failure, so nothing needed for diagnosis is lost. |

## 7. Recommendation

**Adopt: a static red dot in the StatusView in-app (same slot as the yellow pulsing dot, never pulsed, never on the notification) that persists for the whole failure session and clears on the next successful state change to `Playing` (or on new-document load / app restart) — never on a timer.** This is the in-app analogue of the notification's persistent reason line (`reason_ttl_ms=null`): both surfaces stay consistent (notification = reason text, no colour; in-app = reason text + static red dot), and both clear only when the condition resolves.

Residual uncertainty: (i) whether the red dot should also carry a tiny leading glyph on the reason banner (recommend no — keep the banner text neutral for legibility; the dot is the colour carrier); (ii) whether "next successful Play" is detectable before the first successful audio frame (recommend: clear on transition to `Playing` state, not on tap, to avoid a false clear if Play is refused); (iii) the exact on-device verification of the clear transition is not yet in SC-007 and would need a device-leg addition. No "So et al." paper on calibration of persistently-lit warnings was located this pass; the habituation claim rests on the FMVSS 101 non-cancelable rule + the EBSCO/Joint Commission origin case + Bliss & Acton's reliability-calibration finding, which together support "rare + state-locked + cleared by resolution" rather than "always-on red."

## Suggested spec wording

> **FR-005/FR-008 (F4):** The permanent-failure state is carried in-app by a **static red dot** (`--signal-failure` `#FF4D63`) in the same slot as the yellow warning dot — **never** the progress track/fill, never the reason-banner text colour, never on the notification (accent-only). It is **not pulsed** and outlives the 3 s warning TTL (`reason_ttl_ms=null`, mirroring the persistent reason line). It is **latching for the failure session**: it clears only on a successful transition to `Playing`, on a new document load, or on app restart — never on a timer and never by user dismissal while the condition persists (FMVSS 101 §5.5.6(b) — red tell-tales must not be cancelable while the underlying condition exists). The notification surface carries the same failure via its persistent reason line (no colour). Clearing is a change of context and announces resolution to assistive technology; a red that never clears in-app is avoided by construction.
